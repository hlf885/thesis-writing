# Manuscript Writing and Revision Policy

## Applicability and priority

This reference governs **writing and editorial revision only**: argument organization, authorial voice, exposition, scientific claim language, abstract, title, keywords, captions, and reviewer-response prose. It does **not** authorize designing experiments, requesting additional experiments, recalculating statistics, changing numerical results, or performing submission operations. Those tasks require separate user instructions and evidence. If a wording change would alter a scientific statement or create a new factual claim, stop and flag it rather than invent supporting research.

In order of importance: (1) scientific meaning and honest evidential boundaries, (2) the user's requested revision scope, (3) continuity with the original author's voice, (4) clarity and contribution-led argument, and (5) applicable venue language/format requirements. These priorities do not justify preserving objective language errors. A well-written original sentence should survive unchanged.

## 1. Preserve authorial voice and the manuscript's original research framing

Before making edits, examine representative **unchanged** passages, including nearby sections and comparable method, result, and discussion paragraphs when available. Identify the original problem framing, established terminology, sentence rhythm and complexity, paragraph organization, transitions, tone, and level of methodological detail. Treat these as a binding style baseline, not an invitation to imitate the vocabulary of a different journal or model.

Retain accurate and persuasive passages. Avoid systematic synonym substitution, unnatural uniformity, and wholesale rephrasing for an abstract sense of greater formality. Prefer localized corrections. Restructure a paragraph or section only when a real argumentative defect requires it; record the reason. When enough original prose is unavailable, state the limitation and edit conservatively. Preserve any approved manual edits as the current baseline.

## 2. Maintain one contribution-led line of argument

The reader should be able to follow: **research problem -> gap in existing work -> specific contribution -> why the proposed mechanism addresses that gap -> actual findings -> implications**. Use this as a diagnostic chain, not as a mandatory six-sentence or six-section template. The main contribution must be intelligible beyond a standalone list in the abstract or introduction. Method, results, and discussion should concern the same research question. Remove tangents and contradictory framing; do not manufacture claims to fill a perceived gap.

## 3. Write a coherent research narrative, not a development log

Organize the manuscript according to the research argument rather than the chronology of coding, implementation, or revisions. Do not repeatedly narrate 'we later added', 'we then checked', or the history of drafting unless it explains a substantive research decision. Give each paragraph a discernible argumentative function and link it naturally to the next. Describe the essential method and findings in the main text. Supplementary material may contain details but must not be the only place where the central contribution can be understood. Do not turn publication prose into an audit report or change log.

## 4. Give the principal contribution sufficient explanatory space

Allocate prose in proportion to scientific importance. For the central method, explain **why the design is needed, what its key concepts mean, how the parts interact, what differs from prior approaches, and why that difference affects the claimed process or decision**. Compress incidental implementation details, repeated summaries, and nonessential qualifications before compressing the core mechanism. A label, architecture image, or algorithm name is not a substitute for explanation.

## 5. Establish depth through mechanisms, not ornate language

Explanatory depth comes from explicit distinctions, assumptions, dependencies, and operation. For robotics, control, or systems papers, one useful chain is **input/observation -> state update -> decision change -> executed action -> observed or expected consequence**. Use only stages that are actually part of the method. Rather than asserting that a module 'improves reliability', describe which judgment or operation changes, under which relevant conditions, and how that change could support the stated outcome. Distinguish demonstrated consequences from hypotheses. Explain essential equations, pseudocode, and architecture figures in surrounding prose without gratuitously restating them.

## 6. State innovation precisely without promotion

Attribute novelty to identifiable designs, mechanisms, integrations, analyses, or findings. Specify what is inherited from prior work and what is added or changed. Do not treat complexity, a large number of components, or implementation completeness as proof of innovation. Avoid empty labels such as 'powerful framework' and unsupported superlatives such as 'first', 'universally applicable', or 'completely solves'. Do not weaken a valid contribution merely to sound modest; support it through specific differences and evidence already present in the manuscript.

## 7. Preserve essential qualifications while removing defensive repetition

Keep scientific boundaries accurate, concentrated, and close to relevant claims or in a suitable limitations/discussion passage. Remove repeated disclaimers, apologetic phrasing, pre-emptive rebuttals to imagined objections, and redundant self-diminishment only when they add no needed information. Never remove a condition whose omission would overstate the finding. Keep distinct: **not examined**, **not observed under the present conditions**, and **shown ineffective**. Real adverse findings and reviewer-required disclosures are not 'defensive writing' and must not be concealed.

## 8. Separate reporting of results from interpretation

Results prose primarily identifies observations and comparisons. Discussion prose interprets their significance, potential mechanism, relation to prior work, and appropriate limits. If a journal combines Results and Discussion, preserve this logical distinction within paragraphs rather than imposing a different section structure. Avoid copying the same numbers into several sections. Discussion must add analysis beyond paraphrasing results. Label direct observations, supported interpretations, and tentative speculation distinctly. The conclusion summarizes what the manuscript has established and must not introduce a new claim.

## 9. Align title, abstract, keywords, contribution claims, and conclusion

The abstract should efficiently communicate **problem/gap -> specific method -> most informative finding -> significance and necessary boundary**, adjusted to the actual journal's form and word limit if available. Describe the method more concretely than 'a novel framework'. Select findings that illuminate the main contribution without exhaustively listing metrics. Recheck the final abstract's length when the source is accessible; never assume a limit without consulting the target. Use established, searchable technical keywords; avoid promotional or self-invented tags. Ensure the title, abstract, main contribution statement, and conclusion make claims of compatible scope.

## 10. Use precise, natural, economical academic English

Prefer clear grammatical subjects, specific verbs, unambiguous referents, and plain technical vocabulary. Remove hollow openings, unnecessary modifiers, repetition, and awkward constructions. Do not swap a correct common word for a rare synonym merely for perceived sophistication. Do not ban passive voice, long sentences, semicolons, parenthetical phrases, or transitions by category: use them where they aid actual logic and preserve authorial style. Vary sentence openings naturally rather than enforcing diversity. Convert mechanical 'First/Second/Finally' narrative into coherent prose when doing so improves reasoning, but retain numbered algorithms, proof cases, reproducible procedures, and necessary comparisons. 'De-AI-ification' means concrete improvements in accuracy and prose, not artificial errors or stylistic randomness.

## 11. Learn organizational practice from comparable articles, not their wording

When user-provided or legitimately accessible papers from the target journal are available, examine how they motivate the problem, embed contributions, layer method explanations, introduce figures, discuss results, and position limits. Apply useful organizational principles only where they fit this research. Never borrow sentences, disguise paraphrase as original research, or change the method to resemble a template. Venue exemplars are examples, not substitutes for actual journal instructions.

## 12. Treat figure captions, table titles, and terminology as integral prose

Captions and table titles should identify the object, comparison, or role of the displayed item. Explain symbols, abbreviations, and reading conventions when necessary; do not merely reproduce the main text or claim unverified performance. At first substantive mention, guide the reader toward the figure's relevant feature or purpose. Keep naming, abbreviations, symbol capitalization, and method references consistent. Define nonstandard abbreviations at first use where required, and do not rename established terms without reason. Use the separate figure/data audit references only if visual or numerical validation is explicitly in scope.

## 13. Write reviewer responses that are respectful, direct, and traceable

For each reviewer point, aim to explain **how it was understood -> what textual change was made -> why -> where in the revised manuscript**. A short acknowledgement is enough; avoid repetitive thanks, defensiveness, sarcasm, and evasive claims such as 'carefully revised' without specifics. Do not agree with an incorrect criticism merely to appease the reviewer. If disagreeing, provide a civil, evidence-based explanation. When an issue remains unresolved, say what has and has not changed. Claims of completed revision must match the final manuscript. This rule governs response **writing**; it does not authorize additional research tasks.

## 14. Respect the exact editing scope and the author's latest text

'Check only' means diagnose without changing files. A request for local polishing does not authorize whole-paper rewriting. Treat the latest user-approved manual version as canonical. A request to delete one space is not permission to alter nearby capitalization, grammar, punctuation, or terminology. Identify out-of-scope problems in a separate note without silently correcting them. After editing, compare the original and revision to ensure the technical meaning, numerical statements, citations, and boundaries of conclusions have not unintentionally changed.

## Mandatory editorial acceptance pass

Before accepting any revision, verify the following against accessible text:

1. **Voice continuity** — does the edited text still read as the same author's work? Any large rewrite has a documented argumentative need.
2. **Contribution alignment** — does each major paragraph help establish the actual research problem, method, findings, or implications rather than introduce a different topic?
3. **Mechanistic explanation** — are principal design choices intelligible in prose without forcing readers to infer every operation from a figure or equation?
4. **Evidence language** — are observations, interpretations, limitations, and speculation clearly distinguished, without repeated defensiveness or inflated claims?
5. **Section responsibilities** — are abstract, method, results, discussion, conclusion, captions, and reviewer response appropriately distinct and consistent?
6. **Minimality and scope** — revert changes that only decorate the language, preserve approved source text, and report out-of-scope concerns separately.

If a passage already meets these conditions, leave it unchanged. This policy concerns the quality of writing and claims; it does not imply that experiments, equations, or statistical results have been independently validated.
